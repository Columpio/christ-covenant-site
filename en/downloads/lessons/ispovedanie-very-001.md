# 1. Protestant Reformed Church (non-denominational) "The Covenant of Christ"
- CRC (c) "The Covenant of Christ"

## 1) Protestant – from the word protest, disagreement with the direct violation and distortion of the Word of God, the Covenant, Christ by Catholicism and Orthodoxy (five only,Quinque sola)

### (1) Sola Scriptura is only Scripture. 2 Tim. 3:16,1 Pet. 2:9
> All scripture is given by inspiration of God, and is profitable for doctrine, for reproof, for correction, for instruction in righteousness: (2 Tim. 3:16, KJV)
> But ye are a chosen generation, a royal priesthood, an holy nation, a peculiar people; that ye should shew forth the praises of him who hath called you out of darkness into his marvellous light: (1 Pet. 2:9, KJV)

- The Bible is the only inspired and authentic word of the Lord, the only source of Christian doctrines, clear and self-interpreted. (Catholicism, Orthodoxy – traditions are higher than the Scriptures)

### (2) Sola Fide is only faith. Eph. 2:8
> For by grace are ye saved through faith; and that not of yourselves: it is the gift of God: (Eph. 2:8, KJV)

- Forgiveness can be obtained only by faith, regardless of good deeds and deeds – universal sinfulness. (Catholicism, Orthodoxy – salvation can be obtained by faith and works, i.e.
- "the mixed gospel")

### (3) Sola Gratia is only grace. Eph. 2:8
> For by grace are ye saved through faith; and that not of yourselves: it is the gift of God: (Eph. 2:8, KJV)

- Salvation comes only as God's grace, as an undeserved mercy and an undeserved gift from God for Christ's sake, but not as something earned by a sinner. (Catholicism, Orthodoxy – salvation must be earned)

### (4) Solus Christus is only Christ. 1 Tim. 2:5
> For there is one God, and one mediator between God and men, the man Christ Jesus; (1 Tim. 2:5, KJV)

- Jesus Christ is the only Mediator between God and man, salvation is possible only through faith in His Divinity and the All-sufficiency of His Sacrifice. (Catholicism, Orthodoxy– and many other intermediaries)

### (5) Soli Deo gloria – glory to God only. Isa. 42:8,Matt. 4:10
> I am the LORD: that is my name: and my glory will I not give to another, neither my praise to graven images. (Isa. 42:8, KJV)
> Then saith Jesus unto him, Get thee hence, Satan: for it is written, Thou shalt worship the Lord thy God, and him only shalt thou serve. (Matt. 4:10, KJV)

- A person should worship only the Holy Triune God – the Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit, and worship only Him through Jesus Christ as the only visible image of the Invisible God., Since salvation is granted only through His will, the idea is not only the gift of the Atonement of Jesus Christ on the Cross of Calvary, but also the gift of faith in this Atonement, created in the hearts of God's children by the Holy Spirit. (Catholicism, Orthodoxy – worship of people and angels)

## 2) Reformatory (reforms, changes) – the return of the Church and Worship Services to the Original Apostolic model (house churches of up to seventy people are a network, not a pyramid structure of the Church). Acts 2:42
> And they continued stedfastly in the apostles’ doctrine and fellowship, and in breaking of bread, and in prayers. (Acts 2:42, KJV)

### (1) The teaching of the Apostles is the importance of the Church's return to the consistent and systematic study of the Holy Scriptures (the Bible) as the basis of Worship. Luke 24:27, 44-45
> And beginning at Moses and all the prophets, he expounded unto them in all the scriptures the things concerning himself. (Luke 24:27, KJV)
> And he said unto them, These are the words which I spake unto you, while I was yet with you, that all things must be fulfilled, which were written in the law of Moses, and in the prophets, and in the psalms, concerning me. Then opened he their understanding, that they might understand the scriptures, (Luke 24:44-45, KJV)

### (2) The importance of communication and reflection on the Word of God as an obligatory part of Worship

### (3) The importance of the Breaking of Bread as an obligatory part of the Divine Service

## 3) The Church – the importance of understanding the obligatory Confession for the Church of Jesus Christ (the people of God)

### (1) Faith in the Holy Triune God – the Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit revealed in Jesus Christ, i.e. the Divinity of Christ

### (2) Faith in the All–sufficient Sacrifice of Jesus Christ is all the answers and the complete solution to all human problems

## 4) Non–denominational - disagreement with the understanding of the doctrines of the Word of God by the main modern denominations of Protestantism

### (1) Anglicanism (1534) – denial of the forgiveness of all sins, the "head of the church" the King or Queen of England, the belief in partial conversion in Communion

### (2) Presbyterians (1560, Calvinism) – belief in unconditional election, partial salvation (Christ did not die for all people), irresistible grace and the impossibility of falling away from God

### (3) Baptists (1609) – denial of the effects of the gifts of power in the modern era (other languages, miracles, etc.)

### (4) Methodists (Methodist Church, 1720) – belief in partial transfiguration in Communion, faith in complete sanctification, i.e. the possibility of achieving actual holiness on earth in mortal bodies

### (5) Pentecostals (1900) – another tongue as an obligatory gift for every believer

### (6) Charismatics (1960) – the heresy of partial salvation, the destruction of curses and the exorcism of demons from the children of God, the emphasis of believers' lives on visible things (miracles, healings, prosperity), songs and music as the basis of Worship

### (7) Seventh–day Adventists (1860, Sabbaths) - denial of the forgiveness of all sins, observance of the Law as a condition for salvation (Sabbath, pork)

## 5) Protestant Reformed Church (non-denominational) "Covenant of Christ" – proclamation of the Word of God within the framework of Biblical, Covenant and Systematic Theology

### (1) Biblical Theology is a branch of Christian theology that studies the history of Divine revelation as recorded in the scriptures from Genesis to the Book of Revelation

### (2) Covenanted Covenant Theology is a branch of Christian theology that studies the history of God's terms (Covenant, Contract) for the relationship between God and man.

### (3) Systematic (doctrinal, dogmatic) Theology (English Systematic, Dogmatic Theology) is a branch of Christian theology that presents the basic doctrines of Christianity in an orderly and logical manner based on the Holy Scriptures (the Bible)
- Systematic theology is divided into key sections

#### (a) Theology - the doctrine of the Holy, Triune God (nature, qualities and existence of God)

#### (b) Christology – the teaching about Jesus Christ (His Divinity, His Humanity, His Mission)

#### (d) Anthropology – the doctrine of man (Creation, Fall)

#### (e) Soteriology – the doctrine of human salvation

#### (e) Ecclesiology – the teaching of the Church of Jesus Christ, etc.
