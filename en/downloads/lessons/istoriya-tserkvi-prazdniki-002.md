### (4) A.D. 37 – visit to Jerusalem, meeting with Ap. Peter, the intercession of Barnabas, ministry in Jerusalem, persecution, Tarsus, preparation for ministry (Acts 9:26-30; 22:17-21)
> And when Saul was come to Jerusalem, he assayed to join himself to the disciples: but they were all afraid of him, and believed not that he was a disciple. ### (5) 41 A.D. – Peter's calling to the preaching of the Gentiles. (Acts 10)

### (6) 42 A.D. – the beginning of the ministry of Barnabas and Paul the Gentile, Antioch (Syria), the birth of the Antiochian Church (Acts 11:19-26)
> Now they which were scattered abroad upon the persecution that arose about Stephen travelled as far as Phenice, and Cyprus, and Antioch, preaching the word to none but unto the Jews only. And some of them were men of Cyprus and Cyrene, which, when they were come to Antioch, spake unto the Grecians, preaching the Lord Jesus. And the hand of the Lord was with them: and a great number believed, and turned unto the Lord. Then tidings of these things came unto the ears of the church which was in Jerusalem: and they sent forth Barnabas, that he should go as far as Antioch. Who, when he came, and had seen the grace of God, was glad, and exhorted them all, that with purpose of heart they would cleave unto the Lord. For he was a good man, and full of the Holy Ghost and of faith: and much people was added unto the Lord. Then departed Barnabas to Tarsus, for to seek Saul: And when he had found him, he brought him unto Antioch. And it came to pass, that a whole year they assembled themselves with the church, and taught much people. And the disciples were called Christians first in Antioch. (Acts 11:19-26, KJV)

### (9) 48 A.D. – Return to the Antiochian Church and visit to the Jerusalem Church, Paul's account (Gal. 2:1-10)
> Then fourteen years after I went up again to Jerusalem with Barnabas, and took Titus with me also. And I went up by revelation, and communicated unto them that gospel which I preach among the Gentiles, but privately to them which were of reputation, lest by any means I should run, or had run, in vain. But neither Titus, who was with me, being a Greek, was compelled to be circumcised: And that because of false brethren unawares brought in, who came in privily to spy out our liberty which we have in Christ Jesus, that they might bring us into bondage: To whom we gave place by subjection, no, not for an hour; that the truth of the gospel might continue with you. But of these who seemed to be somewhat, (whatsoever they were, it maketh no matter to me: God accepteth no man’s person:) for they who seemed to be somewhat in conference added nothing to me: But contrariwise, when they saw that the gospel of the uncircumcision was committed unto me, as the gospel of the circumcision was unto Peter; (For he that wrought effectually in Peter to the apostleship of the circumcision, the same was mighty in me toward the Gentiles:) And when James, Cephas, and John, who seemed to be pillars, perceived the grace that was given unto me, they gave to me and Barnabas the right hands of fellowship; that we should go unto the heathen, and they unto the circumcision. Only they would that we should remember the poor; the same which I also was forward to do. (Gal. 2:1-10, KJV)

## 7) The history of the Church, the fate of the Apostles

### (1) Peter preached in Jerusalem, Judea, Antioch, Bethany, Asia, Italy, Rome. He was crucified upside down in 67 A.D. by order of Nero

### (2) John the Theologian preached in Jerusalem, Ephesus, and many Asian cities. During the persecution of Christians, he was brought to Rome and sentenced to death. After a bowl of poison and an attempt to boil John in boiling oil, he was exiled to the island of Patmos.
- After a long exile, John was freed and returned to Ephesus, where he died a natural death.

### (3) Andrey – preaching in Greece, Turkey, Crimea, Kiev. He was crucified on an X-shaped cross in the Greek city of Patras.

### (4) James Zavadeev – Sermon in Jerusalem (Epistle of James) beheaded in Jerusalem by order of Herod Agrippa

### (5) Philip – preaching in the Assyrian countries. Crucified upside down in the city of Hierapolis (Turkey)

### (6) Bartholomew – preaching in Asia, India, Armenia
- Martyrdom in the city of Alvan (Armenia)

### (7) Thomas the Sermon in many Asian countries and in India
- Martyrdom in India

### (8) Matthew (the Evangelist) – preaching in Judea and Ethiopia
- Killed by a sword in one of the cities of Ethiopia

### (9) Jacob Alfeev - preaching in Syria and Egypt. Crucified on the cross, martyrdom

### (10) Judas James preached in Judea, Galilee, Samaria, Idumea, Syria, Arabia, and Messopotamia. Hanged on a cross and shot with arrows (Caucasus)

### (11) Simon the Zealot – Preaching in Mauritania and England
- Martyrdom by crucifixion or sawn through with a saw on the Black Sea coast of the Caucasus

### (12) Matthew – preaching in Judea and Ethiopia. Stoned and beheaded in Judea

### (13) Luke preached in Achaia, Egypt, and Libya. Hanged from a tree in the Greek city of Thebes

### (14) Mark (evangelist) – preaching in Alexandria (Egypt), Libya, Africa. Died during the persecution of a crowd of pagans in Alexandria in 68 AD
