(1) Acts 18:1-18, - the appearance of the Corinthian Church

After these things Paul departed from Athens, and came to Corinth; And found a certain Jew named Aquila, born in Pontus, lately come from Italy, with his wife Priscilla; (because that Claudius had commanded all Jews to depart from Rome:) and came unto them. And because he was of the same craft, he abode with them, and wrought: for by their occupation they were tentmakers. And he reasoned in the synagogue every sabbath, and persuaded the Jews and the Greeks. And when Silas and Timotheus were come from Macedonia, Paul was pressed in the spirit, and testified to the Jews that Jesus was Christ. And when they opposed themselves, and blasphemed, he shook his raiment, and said unto them, Your blood be upon your own heads; I am clean: from henceforth I will go unto the Gentiles. And he departed thence, and entered into a certain man’s house, named Justus, one that worshipped God, whose house joined hard to the synagogue. And Crispus, the chief ruler of the synagogue, believed on the Lord with all his house; and many of the Corinthians hearing believed, and were baptized. Then spake the Lord to Paul in the night by a vision, Be not afraid, but speak, and hold not thy peace: For I am with thee, and no man shall set on thee to hurt thee: for I have much people in this city. And he continued there a year and six months, teaching the word of God among them. And when Gallio was the deputy of Achaia, the Jews made insurrection with one accord against Paul, and brought him to the judgment seat, Saying, This fellow persuadeth men to worship God contrary to the law. And when Paul was now about to open his mouth, Gallio said unto the Jews, If it were a matter of wrong or wicked lewdness, O ye Jews, reason would that I should bear with you: But if it be a question of words and names, and of your law, look ye to it; for I will be no judge of such matters. Acts 18:1-18 · KJV

After this, Paul left Athens and went to Corinth. There he found a Jew named Aquila, a native of Pontus, who had recently come from Italy with his wife Priscilla because Claudius had ordered all the Jews to leave Rome. Paul went to visit them, and he stayed and worked with them because they were tentmakers by trade, just as he was. Every Sabbath he reasoned in the synagogue, trying to persuade Jews and Greeks alike. And when Silas and Timothy came down from Macedonia, Paul devoted himself fully to the word, testifying to the Jews that Jesus is the Christ. But when they opposed and insulted him, he shook out his garments and told them, “Your blood be on your own heads! I am innocent of it. From now on I will go to the Gentiles.” So Paul left the synagogue and went next door to the house of Titus Justus, a worshiper of God. Crispus, the synagogue leader, and his whole household believed in the Lord. And many of the Corinthians who heard the message believed and were baptized. One night the Lord spoke to Paul in a vision: “Do not be afraid; keep on speaking; do not be silent. For I am with you and no one will lay a hand on you, because I have many people in this city.” So Paul stayed for a year and a half, teaching the word of God among the Corinthians. While Gallio was proconsul of Achaia, the Jews coordinated an attack on Paul and brought him before the judgment seat. “This man is persuading the people to worship God in ways contrary to the law,” they said. But just as Paul was about to speak, Gallio told the Jews, “If this matter involved a wrongdoing or vicious crime, O Jews, it would be reasonable for me to hear your complaint. But since it is a dispute about words and names and your own law, settle it yourselves. I refuse to be a judge of such things.” And he drove them away from the judgment seat. At this, the crowd seized Sosthenes the synagogue leader and beat him in front of the judgment seat. But none of this was of concern to Gallio. Paul remained in Corinth for quite some time before saying goodbye to the brothers. He had his head shaved in Cenchrea to keep a vow he had made, and then he sailed for Syria, accompanied by Priscilla and Aquila.

(2) The Problems of the Corinthian Church:

(a) Division of the Church, misunderstanding of mission (1-4)

(b) Incest (5)

(c) Conflicts in the Church (6)

(d) Fornication, inaccurate understanding of marriage, family (6-7)

(e) Temptations, participation in idolatry (8.10)

(e) An inaccurate understanding of the Church's economy (9)

(g) Inaccurate understanding of the Participle (11)

(h) Inaccurate understanding of the gifts of the Holy Spirit (12-14)

(i) An inaccurate understanding of the Resurrection (15)

2) 1 Cor. 1-4, - inaccurate understanding of the Word of God, the Covenant, Christ (motives)

(1) Divisions and focus on the person (the leaders) rather than on Jesus Christ, misunderstanding of purpose

(2) The importance of following Jesus Christ, rather than following a person, understanding destiny. – knowledge and transmission of the Word of God, the Covenants, Christ, training and education of disciples, World Evangelization, the 2nd parish of Christ

3) 1 Cor. 5, - the sinful essence of man (incest)

(1) Understanding the causes of human transgressions. (The influence of the remnants of the sinful essence, the world and Satan)

(2) Understanding "sin to death" and "sin not to death", the importance of understanding the Sacrifice of Christ (the Lord's Passover)

(3) God's love and mercy – human education

4) 1 Cor. 6, - inaccurate understanding of the Word of God, the Covenant, Christ (intended)

(a) The cause of conflicts in the Church is idolatry

(b) Conflict resolution in the Church is strictly within the framework of the Word of God

(c) The importance of understanding and fulfilling the mission of the Church

5) 1 Cor. 6-7, - inaccurate understanding of marriage, family (fornication)

(1) The importance of family and marriage – God is the establishment, preservation of man

(2) Acceptance of the Word of God, the Covenant, Christ, destiny. – the right marriage

(3) Understanding the legal and illegal dissolution of marriage

(4) The importance of fulfilling one's destiny (the Gospel. marriage)

6) 1 Corinthians 8:10, - temptations, participation in idolatry

(1) Inaccurate understanding of the Word of God – participation in pagan rituals, seduction of the weak (sinning against Christ)

(2) Participation in idolatry – communion with demons

(3) The importance of living the right life, fulfilling the purpose

7) 1 Cor. 9, - inaccurate understanding of the economics of the Church

(1) The importance of understanding the financial ministry of God's people – tithing, donations, alms

(2) Financial service – works of faith, human participation in the work of God, the importance of understanding and fulfilling the purpose

8) 1 Cor. 11, - inaccurate understanding of the Participle

(1) The importance of understanding Communion is to affirm participation in the Covenant with God through the Sacrifice and Asceticism of Jesus Christ

(2) The importance of reasoning about the Body and Blood of Jesus Christ, His Sacrifice – confidence in the forgiveness of all sins and salvation, the affirmation of the all-sufficiency of the Sacrifice of Jesus Christ and the solution of all human problems (the power of sin, death, curses)

(3) Misunderstanding and misunderstanding of Communion – there is no salvation and rebirth, the importance of accepting the Word of God, the Covenant, Christ

9) 1 Cor. 12-14, - inaccurate understanding of the gifts of the Holy Spirit

(1) The importance of understanding the meaning of the gifts of the Holy Spirit – the knowledge and transmission of the Word of God, the Covenant, Christ, the disciples, Worldwide Evangelization, service to people, the importance of edification and spiritual growth of man

(2) The importance of understanding God's sovereignty – the presence and distribution of the gifts of the Holy Spirit, God's decision and will

(3) The importance of understanding the meaninglessness of the gifts of the Holy Spirit without the action of God's love – service to God and man

(4) The importance of understanding the conformity of the actions of the gifts of the Holy Spirit to the ordinances of the Word of God

10) 1 Cor. 15, - inaccurate understanding of the resurrection

(1) The meaninglessness of Christianity, the sacrifice of Christ without resurrection from the dead – "the victory of sin, death and hell, Satan"

(2) The statement of the absence of resurrection – the humiliation of Christ and His Sacrifice, the misunderstanding of Creation, the Fall, Restoration, the importance of accepting the Word of God, the Covenant, Christ

(3) The importance of understanding the future, the resurrection from the dead, and the life of the next age is God's will for every human being.

11) 1 Cor. 16, - the importance of the disciples and the local Churches, the importance of fulfilling the destiny of the people of God – the knowledge and transmission of the Word of God, the Covenant, Christ, the disciples, Worldwide Evangelization

Conclusion:

(1) Problems of K.C. – problems of modern local churches
