3) Understanding the stages of the history of TS (main events)

(1) The First Apostolic Church – the time of the apostles' lives, 1st century A.D.

(a) The Birth of the Church, the establishment of the Word of God, the Covenant, Christ, the Liturgy of N.Z.

(b) The fullness of the books of Holy Scripture

(c) World Evangelization, disciples, destiny

(d) The destruction of Jerusalem and the Temple in 70 A.D.

(2) The Early Church – 2nd-5th century A.D.

(a) The continuation of the accomplishment of World Evangelization, disciples, destiny

(b) The persecution and persecution of the Church before 313 A.D. – Constantine the Great, the Edict of Milan, the end of the persecution. (The reason for the persecution is the refusal of Christians to worship the emperor as "god incarnate," i.e., the worship of Jesus Christ is a resistance to the "true gods" and the Roman Empire

(c) Ecumenical Councils, condemnation of heresies, affirmation of the Word of God, the Covenant, Christ (the importance of purity of teaching), Canon of N.Z.

(d) 476 A.D. the fall and division of the Roman Empire (Eastern and Western)

(3) Middle (Dark) Ages – 6th-13th century A.D.

(a) Heresies, false teachings, rejection of the Word of God, the Covenant, Christ (the majority), closed access to the Holy Scriptures, the enormous power of religion over the spiritual and secular lives of people

(b) The division of believers into east and west in 1054 AD. (Orthodoxy and Catholicism)

(4) The Reformation – 14th-18th century A.D.

(a) Return to the Word of God, the Covenant, Christ, Protestant Theology (Only Scripture, Only Faith, Only Grace, Only Christ, Glory to God alone – Sola scriptura,Sola fide,Sola gratia, Solus Christus,Soli Deo gloria), open access to the Holy Scriptures for humanity

(b) The continuation of the accomplishment of World Evangelization, disciples, destiny

(5) The Modern Church – 19th-21st century A.D.

(a) The majority are heresies, false teachings, rejection of the Word of God, the Covenant, Christ, lack of study and explanation of the Holy Scriptures

(b) The minority – affirmation of the Word of God, the Covenant, Christ, purity of teaching (only Scripture, only Christ, only Faith, only Grace), study and explanation of the Scriptures, purpose

5) The Theology of Protestantism

(1) Understanding Salvation

(a) Salvation is only through grace and faith – acceptance of the Divinity of Christ and the All-sufficiency of His Sacrifice. (The forgiveness of all sins, the solution of all curses and the power of Satan)

(b) Sanctification is not a condition, but a consequence of salvation – the affirmation of the Word of God, the Covenant, Christ, the right life and the opportunity to serve God

(c) The acceptance of Jesus Christ as the Only Intercessor and Mediator between God and humanity. (Denial of any other mediation – heavenly, human)

(2) Water Baptism and Communion

(a) Water Baptism and Communion confirmation of being in Covenant with God through Jesus Christ (consequence of salvation)

(b) The non–acceptance of the full or partial transfiguration of bread and wine are prototypes of the Body and Blood of Jesus Christ. (Affirmation Of Christ's One-Time Sacrifice, His Resurrection)

(3) Attitude to the Word of God

(a) The Bible is above the traditions and writings of teachers

(b) Translation of the Bible by J. Wycliffe (1382), William Tyndale (1525)

(c) Systematic, consistent study of the Word of God from the perspective of the Covenant, Christ John 5:39

6) A brief analysis of Protestant denominations

  • Protestant Theology – The Reformed Protestant Church, Calvinism and Arminianism. (Other denominations are offshoots, changes, and distortions of Reformation Protestant Theology)

(1) Calvinism and Arminianism. (1509-1564, Jean Calvin, Switzerland, 1560-1609, Jacob Arminius, Holland)

(a) The Divinity of Jesus Christ and the All-sufficiency of His Sacrifice as God's only method of salvation

(b) Calvinism is a specific understanding of God's relationship with humanity (lack of the right to choose, see lesson)

(c) Arminianism is an understanding of God's relationship with humanity from the perspective of the right to choose through the action of God's grace and foreknowledge of human actions and deeds

(2) Baptists (1609, John Smith, Amsterdam)

(a) Accepting the Divinity of Christ, not accepting His All-sufficient Sacrifice. (Mixed gospel – non-acceptance of the forgiveness of all sins through the Sacrifice of Christ, falling away from God through "sin not to death," human deeds as a condition for salvation, approximately 50%)

(b) Denial of the gifts and the Baptism of the Holy Spirit (extreme)

(3) Adventists, Sabbatarians (1860, Ellen White, USA)

(a) Accepting the Divinity of Christ, not accepting His All-sufficient Sacrifice. (Non-acceptance of the forgiveness of all sins through the Sacrifice of Christ)

(b) The Mixed Gospel – observing the Sabbath and eating pork as a condition for human salvation

(4) The Pentecostals (1900, Charles Parham, USA)

(a) Accepting the Divinity of Christ, not accepting His All-sufficient Sacrifice. (Mixed gospel – non-acceptance of the forgiveness of all sins through the Sacrifice of Christ, falling away from God through "sin not to death," human deeds as a condition for salvation, approximately 75%, belief in the possibility of curses and possession of the people of God)

(b) Strong emphasis on gifts and the Baptism of the Holy Spirit as a condition for salvation (extreme)

(5) Charismatics (1960, Dennis Bennett, USA)

(a) Accepting the Divinity of Christ, not accepting His All-sufficient Sacrifice. (Mixed gospel – non-acceptance of the forgiveness of all sins through the Sacrifice of Christ, falling away from God through "sin not to death," human deeds as a condition for salvation, approximately 75%)

(b) The rejection of salvation of the human soul and body through the Sacrifice of Christ, the heresy of "partial salvation," the belief in the possibility of curses and possession of the people of God

(c) Strong emphasis on gifts and the Baptism of the Holy Spirit (extreme)

6) False Protestant denominations

(1) Lutherans (1517, Martin Luther, Germany, Augsburg Confession, June 25, 1530)

(a) Martin Luther is a Catholic reformer, but not a Protestant

(b) The loss of salvation through sin does not lead to death. (Non-acceptance of the forgiveness of all sins through the Sacrifice of Christ)

(c) Confession as a condition for the forgiveness of sins and the restoration of human salvation

(d) Water Baptism as a condition for salvation

(e) The belief in partial transfiguration in Communion

(2) Jehovah's Witnesses, Arianism

  • (4th century A.D., priest Arius and 1870, Charles Russell, USA)

(a) Denial of the Trinity of the Holy, True God

(b) The relationship to Jesus Christ is a created person, Archangel Michael in the form of a man. (Denial of the Divinity of Christ and the All-Sufficiency of His Sacrifice)

(c) Deeds and deeds as a condition for human salvation

(3) The Mormons (1830, Joseph Smith, USA)

(a) Denial of the Trinity of God, belief in three different and separate gods – heresy of tretheism, polytheism, belief not in the God of the Bible

(b) God the Father – at first was a man, and then became god as a result of the process of "eternal development" (created being), and also has a carnal body

(c) Jesus Christ – in the beginning was a man, and then became God as a result of the process of "eternal development" (created being).

(d) Every human being must become a god through a personal process of "eternal development" and there are already many gods who used to be human.

(e) Equating human books with the Bible. (Book of Mormon, Doctrine and Covenants, Pearl of Great Price)
